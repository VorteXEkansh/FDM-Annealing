# Which motion closes a clearance?

The plates do not define the hot clearance by themselves. A stop bearing between the inner faces expands the opening, whereas plates expanding inward from fixed outer faces reduce it. A fixture that actively holds its inner spacing fixed gives a third reference. These are alternative design boundary conditions; none was measured on an actual fixture. Treating the fixture as rigid while prescribing its hot inside spacing still requires a choice among these kinematics.

Let h and g be the initial specimen thickness and total free clearance, b the thickness of each plate, and U the change in the unloaded inside spacing. For a flat, centered and uniformly heated coupon with small axial strain, opposing-face engagement requires e ≥ (g + U)/h − αₚΔT, with e the **signed** nonthermal free thickness strain. Growth aids contact and contraction opposes it. Equality means first touch, not finite reaction. This screen requires neither a guessed e nor a fictitious initial stress. For U = 0 the spacing is fixed; for inside spacers U = (h + g)εₛ; for fixed outer faces U = −2bεₛ. The steel reference uses the 20–100 °C interval mean from Outokumpu Table 7 (p. 10) as constant across the smaller temperature interval, rebased to 23 °C. This is an engineering approximation, not an interpretation of the unresolved convention in Meng's earlier table. The finite change is evaluated with reference dimensions, consistent with a small-strain screen. The three fixtures are not uncertainty bounds.

The code reads the original design geometry, Chapuis PLA expansion coefficient and manufacturer steel register. At 80 °C and 0.020 mm clearance the critical recovery strains for fixed inside spacing, steel inside spacers and fixed outer faces are +0.1124%, +0.2041% and −0.1156%. A flat part with zero nonthermal thickness change would be separated for the first two support arrangements and in contact for the third. This is a conditional comparison under identical uniform specimen/fixture temperatures; it does not establish the contact status of an oven-treated part. At the same temperature and 0.010 mm clearance, a contraction exceeding 0.0462% is sufficient to avoid closure with the steel inner spacers. If thickness increases instead, closure is easier. There is no basis here to transfer the sign of longitudinal shrinkage to the thickness direction.

On cooling, the thermal term recedes; even a formerly closed interface can unload and separate. At return to reference temperature a flat unconstrained shape spanning both faces would require e ≥ g/h. Release removes the pressure boundary condition. A symmetric, homogeneous, stress-free printed state under symmetric heating supplies no independent source of bending; this does not rule out buckling or measured printing imperfections. A bowed part can contact one face before the flat thickness criterion is met. Pressure, area and released shape then require a surface problem with measured initial shape and transport, a credible constitutive history, plate bending compliance and support stiffness. The stop benchmark isolates onset with rigid parallel faces. It does not include those effects.

For future 3-D comparisons use the same predefined top face, sampled at the same material positions on a fixed quadrature grid. Orthogonal least squares minimizes squared perpendicular distances to one best-fit plane. Equal weights implement the stated nodal RMS; supply area weights for a separately labelled area-based metric on nonuniform meshes. Retain peak absolute distance and RMS. The sign of the fitted normal is arbitrary. For directional dimensional gauges use Euclidean separations of predefined corresponding opposite-face fiducials (x, y, z material axes at the reference state); do not rotate an axis-aligned bounding box after deformation. An experiment measured with callipers may need a different measurement operator. `analysis/surface_metrics.py` and its tests establish rigid-motion invariance, not coupon metrology validation. No production residual state was observed.
