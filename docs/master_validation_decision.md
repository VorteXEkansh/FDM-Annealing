# Physical validation decision — 31 October 2026

**No independent physical prediction–measurement pair is admitted for a released, gap-constrained Prusament coupon.** This is a failure to identify compatible evidence, not a result of zero validation error. Preserve the frozen calibration and validation registers; no new fit was made and no reserved observation was used to choose numerical coefficients. The benchmark error against an analytical solution cannot enter a physical-validation error table.

## Why a simple fitted shrinkage curve is inadequate

The reserved [Lluch-Cerezo et al. Table 5](https://doi.org/10.3390/polym14132607) supplies six sets of final directional means for **one** hold duration, 120 min. Even a scalar saturation law has a non-identifiability: at a single duration, changing the assumed relaxation time can be compensated by changing the fitted saturation magnitude. Varying temperature at that same duration need not identify their separate temperature dependences. Width and height are not independent replications and the final dimensions contain no measurement of internal stress. Assigning the observations as eigenstrain inputs and subsequently scoring the same dimensions is circular. Fitting a curve to one subset without a documented protocol change violates the frozen reservation. The full register contains no measured response to the proposed opposed plates.

An empirical model of terminal strain could reasonably be scoped to a single grade, architecture, cycle, support and observation delay, provided separate calibration and testing specimens exist. That route can interpolate an endpoint; it cannot infer transient onset or time-dependent contact, released stress, or a clearance-dependent processing optimum from endpoints alone. Alternatively, a prescribed measured part temperature can bypass a missing heat-transfer law for the matched experiment. It cannot justify a comparison at a common oven schedule, and it still requires the recovery law and independent observations.

## Evidence that changes the decision

1. Obtain independent calibration on a specified filament and toolpath: directional expansion on a repeat cycle, first-cycle directional recovery at more than one duration and temperature, and initial curvature/through-thickness print state. Use measured part temperatures; separate reversible/delayed recovery from permanent change at a declared observation delay.
2. Reserve a separate build or independent publication, before fitting, with before/after landmarks or surface scans, actual print settings, part/plate temperatures, support, release history and measured clearance. Repeatability and instrument resolution must refer to the measured observable. Record any dropout instead of redefining the domain to match outcomes.
3. Validate FREE dimensional directions separately; evaluate GAP/flatness separately on the reserved fixture experiment. Define MAE/RMSE in percentage points or millimetres as appropriate. Predeclare group and bound interpretation; no inference based on repeated time steps or multiple surfaces of one print as independent experiments.
4. Contact onset requires a direct gap/contact or reaction measurement versus time; local pressure requires an appropriate spatial/area reference. Residual stress requires a stress-sensitive observation or independent release/inverse experiment with uncertainty. Dimensional agreement supplies neither. Without those observations label the outputs conditional, regardless of solver quality.
