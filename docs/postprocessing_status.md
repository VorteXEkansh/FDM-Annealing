## Comparison rules that remain unexercised

FREE and GAP comparisons require the same material, geometry, printing architecture, thermal schedule, numerical fidelity and cooled/released observation state, differing only in the declared restraint. Changing temperature or holding time requires otherwise matched cases. Equal oven programs do not establish equal specimen histories. Hot in-fixture dimensions cannot substitute for released residual dimensions. No such pairs are available.

Absolute changes need actual response pairs; percentage changes also need a meaningful nonzero baseline and a stated sign convention. A missing or zero reference is not grounds to invent a suppression percentage. Observed signed dimension changes must not be replaced by unsigned magnitudes. ## Mechanistic interpretation requires discrimination

The manuscript's existing governing relations define possible mechanisms; they do not demonstrate that any mechanism caused a production response. Thermal expansion requires actual temperature/displacement histories; cooling and fixture expansion require their histories and a valid fixture expansion convention. Stress relaxation requires a verified history-dependent constitutive implementation and separation from changing restraint or modulus. Irreversible shrinkage requires an independently parameterized law or supported initial state, neither supplied by reversible expansion alone. The model must not double-count stiffness reduction and relaxation.

Contact onset requires gap closure, contact status, pressure, penetration and time-resolution evidence together. A nominal gap alone does not identify an onset temperature. Lower hot displacement is not evidence of lower released warpage, and higher contact pressure is not evidence of dimensional benefit. Dimensional agreement alone cannot establish residual-stress validity. None of these connections can be asserted as an observed campaign mechanism without fields, histories and matched comparisons.

If a future trend is unexpected, first inspect the exact immutable input, temperature history, material-validity range, solver warnings, contact state/penetration, rigid-body stabilization, units, extraction region and cooling/release state. Distinguish discretization/extraction problems from plausible physical behavior before interpretation. Retain inconvenient values and failed attempts; any correction must create a new traceable derived artifact or run, never overwrite raw output.
