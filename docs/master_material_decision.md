# Material decision and identifiability of annealing recovery

Evidence cutoff: 31 October 2026. This audit supplements rather than replaces the immutable material database. See `literature/master_search.json` for access, locators, DOI checks and the limits of the search.

**Decision.** Select C for the verified Prusament constitutive component: keep actual material-point temperatures between 23 and 85 °C; the new programmed-road calculation uses only 23–75 °C. Neither is a physically validated temperature interval for a coupon. No bulk production material is admitted. The original 95 and 110 °C proposals are outside the measured DMA range. Observation at a higher temperature on a particular object does not extend the relaxation spectrum, crystallization state or irreversible strain law to all coupons. In particular, restricting temperature does not identify the as-printed state.

**Property basis.** Use `material/pla_properties.csv` and its source/uncertainty registers for published values and formulation restrictions. The Prusament equilibrium modulus and 23 branch moduli/times represent the rate and temperature dependence with the piecewise clock. An independent static modulus multiplier would duplicate or alter that dependence. Use only the source constant Poisson ratio and reversible expansion coefficient as constitutive assumptions; no complete measured orthotropic matrix, directional expansion law or density curve is asserted. Supplier density is a screening constant without a test temperature. No temperature-dependent thermal conductivity or heat capacity was admitted for this Prusament printing condition in the search. No numerical interval is assigned where no compatible bound was found. The Ultrafuse and Black Devil results correct the stronger but unjustified statement that thermal and directional-strain data for printed PLA in general do not exist.

## What the source actually identifies

Chapuis et al. calibrated a process-specific *active-layer* strain from the **curvature** of 25 × 5 mm specimens containing four passive and three active 0.1 mm layers (six specimens per condition). Their 195 °C nozzle, 75 °C activation example is represented by axial prestrain 0.056175 in the published spring input (0.05618 rounded in Table 5). The value is not a measured free axial shrinkage of our plate. The companion code initializes the equilibrium spring with a tensile stress and individual Maxwell arms in proportionally opposing compression. The initial resultant vanishes; the internal state is not relaxed. As the arm stresses decay, the free road contracts toward its programmed stress-free length. This is stored-strain recovery, not a universal crystallization or annealing eigenstrain. It can be reprogrammed; calling it irreversible without specifying the thermal/mechanical cycle would misstate the physics.

For uniaxial reduction let p be calibrated axial prestrain; ε is subsequent axial mechanical strain relative to the printed state. Initially, h₀ = E∞ p, hⱼ = −h₀ Eⱼ / ΣEⱼ. During each segment, the equilibrium branch gains E∞ Δε; each other arm becomes Aⱼ hⱼ + Eⱼ Bⱼ Δε, with Aⱼ = exp(−rⱼ), Bⱼ = (1 − Aⱼ)/rⱼ and rⱼ the reduced-time increment divided by its reference relaxation time. Summing these quantities and imposing zero end traction determines Δε. The approximation is piecewise linear strain in reduced time; the temperature-dependent clock is integrated separately. Total axial strain adds α(T − Tᵣ). At long reduced time all Maxwell stresses decay, so the mechanical free strain approaches −p. A second recovery eigenstrain for this same initialization would count its release twice. No 3-D as-printed tensor has been inferred from p.

The Python implementation in `src/programmed_recovery.py` is derived from force balance. It does not port the original UMAT or use its simulation predictions as laboratory data. `scripts/check_programmed_recovery.py` generates the phase-endpoint and refinement register, with source/code hashes in `reproducibility/programmed_road_manifest.json`. In the single-arm limit the independent solution has a retardation time (E∞ + E₁)τ₁/E∞; the numerical tests check approach to that solution, the stress sign, balance, thermal reversibility and temperature guards. With the source coefficient in the 23-arm example, the end-heating mechanical strain at the finest discretization is −0.056136. After the 10 s hold and cool-down it approaches −0.056175 as the force-balance limit predicts. This is a Class B material-point calculation. Neither value is a measured shrinkage or a bulk ANSYS result.

## Spring validation: separate cohorts, incompatible thermal and material assumptions

The public spring mesh and example input permit replication of the *authors' model*, a geometrically nonlinear layered shell with kinematic connections, radial/tangential material axes, and alternate 0.3/0.1/0.3 mm active/carrier/passive groups. The input provides 2,880 shell elements in the paper's Table 3, not a bulk coupon. The cited source sets identical material spectra for active and passive layers but different programmed prestrain. The spring deck prescribes spatially uniform nodal temperatures (22→75 °C in 30 s, 10 s at 75 °C, 10 s cooling to 22 °C). They are not measured oven or part histories. The project material-point check starts and ends at 23 °C instead, remains within the DMA range and does not predict spring height or force.

Tracing the mean deployed height to the **original experiment** changes the assessment. Chapuis and Shea (2023), DOI 10.1016/j.matdes.2023.112163, §§2.1.4, 3.1, Table 2, report 15 multiple-turn springs, 31.9 mm mean and 2.29 mm standard deviation. The experiment used *Prusament PLA Galaxy Silver* for the active ply, *Dutch Filament PLA Dark Blue* for the passive one, a dissolvable support and a circulated-water bath at 70 °C for 40 s. The independent force-test cohort comprised five springs per type (§3.2); the later paper reports 43.69 N at 40% compression in §3.2.2. The earlier height mean is repeated, not a new independent second height dataset. No specimen-by-specimen curves accompany the supplied UMAT/input/video archive. An observation traceable to an original mean can still be useful, but different materials and bath histories may dominate the discrepancy. It is wrong to compare free uniaxial strain with spring height, or to mark the later authors' FE height (32.82 mm) and force (40.81 N) as new ANSYS or physical observations.

Consequently the separate cohorts are not circular with the bilayer-curvature calibration, but the quantities are not predictions of the one-dimensional road model. `validation/master_spring_targets.csv` reserves them without model predictions or metrics. Full spring replication would require matched laminate orientations, the actual passive material, connector and load kinematics, an understood thermal history, and a model of beam/shell action. Even a successful comparison would test the structural response of the programmed laminate; it would not establish contact/warpage/stress validity for a homogeneous opposed-plate coupon. No bulk model is released for optimization on this basis.

## Novelty and next evidence

Lorenzoni et al. already annealed PLA between opposed plates; precise plate clearance alone is not a contribution. Chapuis et al. modeled print-programmed viscoelastic deployment and compared force and shape to experiments. Bute et al. linked directional thermal strain with deposition simulations. The Frölich group characterized anisotropic reorientation and cold crystallization and predicted process-induced deformation (DOI 10.1016/j.addma.2025.105023). Ben Amor and Souissi (DOI 10.1007/s43939-026-00769-2) combined multiscale calculation, experimental validation and optimization of annealed PLA tensile behavior. Lakshmaiya (DOI 10.1016/j.rineng.2026.110279) used experimental recycled-PLA data, a physics-informed surrogate and optimization. These last two were already in the historical bibliography; they were independently retrieved, not newly discovered. No exclusive priority claim follows from this targeted search. Room-temperature sliding-friction values for steel contacts, e.g. DOI 10.2478/msp-2025-0039, do not identify near-static friction or pressure-dependent conductance of hot Prusament against a specified finish.

To admit a specific plate, fix formulation, pigment, moisture/aging history, geometry, printing path and initial curvature; acquire signed free directional strain versus measured specimen temperature and time during several independent ramps/holds and an unloaded second cycle to separate reversible expansion. Record through-thickness heterogeneity rather than assuming it. Obtain matched thermal properties or measured part/plate temperatures, fixture stop expansion, clearance/tolerance, surface condition, contact conductance/friction and plate compliance. Reserve whole new FREE and known-gap treatment groups *before* fitting; record post-release dimensions and surface height and, if claiming stress or pressure accuracy, an independent observation for that response. Restricted claims are viable without these measurements; validated optimization of this bulk fixture is not established by the records inspected.
